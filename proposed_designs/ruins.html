Chronicle · VII

On the Nature of Walls That Remember

Stone does not forget. Each crack is a record — a season of frost, a siege, a century of wind pressing insistently from the east. What we call ruins are, in truth, the most honest buildings of all: structures that no longer pretend at wholeness, that wear their age as a plain fact rather than an embarrassment.

Manuscript · III

The Cartographer Who Mapped What No Longer Existed

Fra Benedetto spent forty years mapping the borders of a kingdom that had ceased to exist before he was born. He worked from accounts, from legends, from the uncertain memories of very old men. When he died, his maps were the most detailed in the known world — for places that could no longer be reached.

Folio · XII

A Bestiary of Creatures Found Only in Abandoned Places

There are animals that thrive only in the spaces left behind by human habitation: in the arched cellars of monasteries, in the wells of depopulated villages, in the corridors of keeps whose lords have been dust for generations. They are the inheritors of our abandonment, and they are utterly indifferent to what we built.
